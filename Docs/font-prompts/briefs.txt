=== Script | Elegant connected script ===
A flowing, elegant connected script with a gentle forward slant of about 12 degrees, like fast but careful pen lettering. Medium contrast: thin upstrokes, thicker downstrokes, round terminals. Lowercase letters join each other at the baseline with smooth entry and exit strokes, so words read as one continuous line, and every joined pair (including ff, fi, th, tt) has to connect cleanly. Capitals are larger and more ornate but stay legible on their own (a lyric line often starts with one), with modest swashes that never run into the next letter. Use OpenType contextual alternates ("calt") if that makes the joins better, but the font must also look right in apps that ignore them. Numerals are simple, upright old-style figures. Mood: romantic, warm, late-night ballad. It must stay readable at 28 pt, so keep the x-height generous and the loops open.

=== Brush | Dry brush marker ===
Bold capitals and lowercase drawn with a wide dry brush or chisel marker: strong thick-and-thin variation from the brush angle, slightly ragged edges where the bristles drag, and a few dry-brush streaks inside the strokes. Energetic, hand-made, confident, like a hand-painted poster or a punk album cover. Letters are upright with a slight lean, tightly spaced, and sit on an uneven but believable baseline (keep the actual metrics at the baseline; the unevenness lives inside each glyph). Keep the rough texture to the edges and a few streaks only, so the letterforms stay clear at 28 pt and the file stays small. Numerals and punctuation match, with round, fat full stops.

=== Tag | Street tag lettering ===
Original street-style tag lettering made legible: thick, rounded-corner marker strokes with a slight forward lean and an unmistakable hand-written rhythm, a little bounce between letters, small arrow-like or hook-like terminals, and a subtle drop-shadow-style offset OUTLINE built into each glyph (a thin outline offset to the lower right, as part of the outline, not a second layer). Playful and loud, like a mural. Capitals and lowercase are both full-size so words look like a tag; letters never connect. It must be readable by anyone, not an unreadable wildstyle: every letter is clearly itself. Do not imitate any real artist's tag or lettering.

=== Gothic | Modern blackletter ===
A modern, simplified blackletter in the Textura spirit: angular, broken strokes with diamond-shaped terminals, strong vertical rhythm, tight spacing and a high x-height so it stays readable. Strong thick-thin contrast from a broad nib held at about 45 degrees. Capitals are bold and decorated with flat cuts and hairline accents but remain legible on their own (many readers struggle with blackletter capitals, so design them to be unambiguous: A, E, G, H, M, S, T, U, V, W). Lowercase letters share one consistent construction. Numerals are plain and clear. Mood: heavy metal poster, tattoo flash, old newspaper masthead, but clean enough for a lyric line.

=== Saloon | Wild-west wood type ===
A wild-west wood-type display face: tall, condensed, heavy slab-serif capitals with tuscan flared spurs at the middle of strokes, bracketed serifs, a decorative inline cut in the thick strokes of the largest letters, and slightly rough, inked corners. Lowercase letters follow the same style at about 70 percent of the capital height (small-cap-like is fine). Think old posters, "wanted" notices and saloon signs, but cleaned up to be sharp on a screen. Strong vertical stress and a tall, narrow proportion so long lyric lines still fit across the screen. Numerals are tall slab figures with the same flared spurs.

=== Hand | Casual handwriting ===
Natural, casual handwriting, as if a thoughtful person wrote lyrics in a notebook with a fine felt pen: slightly irregular, upright to a light lean, rounded, with believable variation. Provide at least two alternate shapes for the most common letters (a, e, o, s, t, r, n, i, l, d) via OpenType contextual alternates ("calt") or "salt"-style cycling, so repeated letters do not look stamped, and make it still look acceptable if those features are ignored. Single-storey a and g, an open friendly e, simple loops. A dotted i with a slightly off-centre round dot, hand-drawn numerals, and punctuation that looks pen-drawn. Mood: intimate, honest, diary-like. Must stay clear at 28 pt.

=== Bubble | Inflated bubble letters ===
Fat, inflated, balloon-like rounded letters with soft round counters, very thick uniform strokes that look puffy and 3D-ish through their outline shape alone, generous round terminals, and a playful bounce, as in a candy wrapper or a bubblegum-pop lyric video. Capitals and lowercase are both chunky; counters (the holes in a, e, o, B, 8) must stay open enough to read at 28 pt, so keep a clear minimum counter size. Letters are tightly spaced but never touching. Numerals and punctuation are equally puffy, with big round dots on i, j, ! and ?. Mood: cheerful, glossy, sugary.

=== Retro | 80s retro sci-fi italic ===
A wide, fast, 80s retro sci-fi italic: extended proportions, a forward slant of about 14 degrees, medium-heavy strokes, and a signature horizontal cut through the middle of each letter (a thin slit across the lower third, as part of the outlines) like a classic synthwave logo. Rounded outer corners with sharp inner corners, smooth curves, geometric construction. Lowercase is a small-cap style at about 78 percent height. Numerals are wide and geometric with the same slit. Mood: neon highway, night drive, arcade poster. Make sure the slits don't make letters like E, F, B and 8 ambiguous at 28 pt.

=== Forge | Heavy metal jagged ===
A heavy-metal display face: thick, aggressive strokes whose OUTER EDGES are jagged and serrated like blades or broken glass, spiked and pointed terminals, angular joints, sharp diagonal cuts, and the occasional barb curling off a letter, symmetrical and strong. The inner counters stay clean and open. It must still read instantly: it is for lyrics, not for unreadable band logos, so keep the word shapes clear, keep spikes from colliding with neighbours, and keep the overall letter silhouettes simple underneath the aggression. Capitals only is acceptable; lowercase may be small caps of the same design. Numerals are heavy and angular. Mood: metal album cover, forged iron, flames.

=== Blade | Razor sharp italic ===
Sharp, fast, slanted capitals whose strokes taper to razor-fine points at their ends, like knives or katana blades. High contrast between the thick middle of a stroke and its needle-thin tips; a slant of about 16 degrees; long angled terminals and pointed joins. Narrow-to-medium width, so lines fit. Lowercase is a small-cap style of the same design. Numerals follow the same taper. Mood: swift, dangerous, elegant, like a martial-arts movie title. Make sure the sharp tips never overlap into neighbouring letters at normal spacing.

=== Stencil | Military stencil ===
A heavy, geometric military stencil face: strong, wide, mostly straight strokes with rounded-square corners, and clean bridges (gaps) cut into letters where counters would otherwise fall out, like stencilled crates and vehicle markings. Bridges are consistent in width and placed in a predictable system (A, B, D, O, P, Q, R, 0, 4, 6, 8, 9 and so on), thin enough to read as stencil gaps yet wide enough to survive blur and glow. Lowercase is a small-cap style at about 80 percent height. Mood: tough, industrial, tactical.

=== Circuit | Techno monoline ===
A clean techno monoline face built only from horizontal, vertical and 45-degree lines with chamfered (cut) corners instead of curves, like a circuit board trace or a sci-fi dashboard. A uniform stroke weight, small square terminals, and the occasional notch or break in the stroke for character. Extended proportions, even spacing, geometric construction. Lowercase is a small-cap style at about 80 percent height. Numerals are rectilinear with chamfers; 0 has a slash or dot so it differs from O. Mood: futuristic interface, hacker, synth.

=== Neon | Neon tube signage ===
A smooth monoline face that looks like bent glass neon tubing: a uniform medium stroke, fully rounded caps, curves everywhere possible, and letters that look like they could be made from one continuous bent tube (a few single-stroke gaps where the tube would pass behind itself are welcome, but every letter must read cleanly). Slightly rounded-geometric proportions, generous spacing so a glow around it does not merge neighbouring letters. Lowercase is a friendly rounded design at normal x-height. Numerals are simple and round. Mood: bar sign at night, retro diner.

=== Pixel | Chunky bitmap ===
A chunky bitmap-style face drawn on a strict square pixel grid (7 pixels tall for capitals, a 5-wide standard width, proportional spacing), with each pixel a perfect square in the outline so it stays crisp at any size. Provide true lowercase letters on the same grid (5 pixels tall with descenders), all required punctuation and accents, and numerals with a slashed or dotted zero. Merge adjacent pixels into clean outlines so there are no hairline seams. Mood: classic arcade and early computer.

=== Thorn | Gothic barbed caps ===
Tall, thin, spiky gothic-leaning capitals with fine barbs and thorn-like hooks growing from stems and terminals, high stroke contrast, and elegant, vertical proportions with a slight sense of danger and romance, like vines or ironwork. Strokes are thin but not hairline: keep them thick enough to read at 28 pt. Lowercase is a small-cap style at about 78 percent height. Numerals are tall and thin with small barbs. Mood: dark romance, gothic, witchy.
